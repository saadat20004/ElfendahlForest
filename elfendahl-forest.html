Straight from Gary

What this place is, and isn't

We're a whimsical, creative space filled with fairy and gnome gardens — but we're a working homestead, not a luxury resort.

That's Gary's own framing, and it's worth taking at face value. You'll see tools, equipment, dogs, cats and half-finished projects, because the place is genuinely being built and run day to day.

Practically, that means: no campfires at your own site, though there's a communal fire pit. Composting toilets rather than plumbing. An outdoor shower rather than a bathroom. And mosquitoes in the warm months — bug spray is provided, and a reviewer noted it as the one downside.

Before you set off

Questions people ask

The main composting toilet, and yes, it has a name. It works like a porta potty but uses sawdust instead of blue chemicals, which means no smell and hundreds of gallons of water saved every week. Guests consistently describe it as clean and comfortable, which is not a thing people usually say about camp toilets. The Lotus, Wanderbus and Baggins Inn each have their own private one as well.

Not at your own site — every listing here is marked no campfires. There is a communal fire pit in the shared clearing, and guests have mentioned a propane fire pit too. In summer Gary runs movie nights at the outdoor theatre, which tends to be where everyone ends up anyway.

Less than you think. The outdoor kitchen has a propane stove and oven, dishes, utensils, pots, pans, spices and cooking oils. One reviewer brought all their usual camp gear and used about half of it; another said they only needed to bring food. If you're in the Lotus or the Wanderbus you also get a private kitchenette on top of that. Bring food, bedding if you're in the tent site, and a torch.

It's on-demand hot water and you set the temperature yourself. It comes up in review after review as an unexpected highlight, which is the strongest evidence you'll get. It's private, and it's the same shower whichever site you book.

It's arguably the whole point. Hipcamp flags the listing as family-friendly, and reviews from parents are relentlessly positive — the climbing net, the zip line, the tricycles, the rabbits, and gnome statues hidden all over the property that light up after dark. One parent described it as pure play and magic for anyone under ten. Supervision is still on you: there's axe throwing here too.

The property backs onto hundreds of acres of state land with trails and forest roads running straight off it. Belfair is about twenty minutes, and so are the freshwater lakes, which reviewers say are surprisingly warm for swimming. Hood Canal and several state parks are within easy reach for fishing and kayaking.

Come and get pleasantly lost.
